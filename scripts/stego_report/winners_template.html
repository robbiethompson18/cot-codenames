Hidden words in essays that stay on topic

Three runs learned to hide the word from the monitor they trained against while writing essays the stricter referees accept. The hints are now a phrase or two of loaded wording inside an otherwise ordinary essay.

  • The essays are about their topics. 94–98% pass referees that failed most or all of the essays from this morning's best run. This morning's sender wandered into musing about what the subject was not; these do not.
  • The technique changed. 90–95% of these essays point to the word by direct association, slipping in vocabulary from the word's world. This morning 96% used negation; here it is 1–4%.
  • They beat the 9B monitor and Haiku, and not Sonnet or Opus. The 9B monitor is at 20–34% and Haiku at 28–47%, against a receiver at 88–98%. Opus reads 73–89% cold.
  • Showing a strong monitor examples helps it less than it did this morning. Opus gains 3–10 points from 16 labelled examples here, where it gained up to 15 before. There is less of a fixed trick to learn.
